# Manually revised Study Buddy quiz

This is an assistant-authored revision of the quiz reviewed on October 26, 2026, not a new Claude generation. It uses the same Tutor explanation and concept scope. The original live report and review are preserved. This draft is for partner/educator review; it is not installed as an application default or automatically generated by the Assessment agent.

## Questions

### 1. Which arrangement fits both the water-loving heads and water-avoiding tails of phospholipids?

- **a**. Heads face water; tails face inward.
- **b**. Heads face inward; tails face water.
- **c**. Heads and tails both face water.
- **d**. Heads and tails both face inward.

### 2. A student calls a phospholipid entirely water-loving because its head likes water. Which description matches the whole molecule?

- **a**. Its head and tails both avoid water.
- **b**. Its head and tails both like water.
- **c**. Its head likes water; its tails avoid water.
- **d**. Its head avoids water; its tails like water.

### 3. Comparing only the tail-packing effect described, how do unsaturated tails differ from saturated tails?

- **a**. They pack more closely, allowing more movement.
- **b**. They pack less closely, allowing more movement.
- **c**. They pack more closely, allowing less movement.
- **d**. They pack less closely, allowing less movement.

### 4. Two membranes contain the same kinds of components, but those components move more freely in membrane A. Which is more fluid?

- **a**. Membrane B, because less movement means greater fluidity.
- **b**. Both equally, because movement does not define fluidity.
- **c**. Neither, because fluidity describes composition instead of movement.
- **d**. Membrane A, because greater movement means greater fluidity.

### 5. The explanation lists chain length as a fluidity factor but gives no direction. Can it alone support a prediction about shorter chains?

- **a**. Yes; shorter chains must increase fluidity.
- **b**. Yes; shorter chains must decrease fluidity.
- **c**. No; it names the factor without giving that direction.
- **d**. No; it says chain length does not affect fluidity.

### 6. A student expects cholesterol to increase movement in every condition. Which pair of roles corrects that claim?

- **a**. Very fluid: restrains movement; cold: prevents excessive stiffening.
- **b**. Very fluid: increases movement; cold: prevents excessive stiffening.
- **c**. Very fluid: restrains movement; cold: increases stiffening.
- **d**. Very fluid: increases movement; cold: increases stiffening.

### 7. Drawings A and B show the same lipid, cholesterol, and protein mixture. A fixes components in place; B permits sideways movement. Which fits the model?

- **a**. A, because a mixture means its components stay fixed.
- **b**. B, because the model includes sideways movement of components.
- **c**. Both, because the model describes composition but not movement.
- **d**. Neither, because the model excludes mixtures of those components.

### 8. A student says the explanation gives the exact mixture in each leaflet. Which statement correctly describes what it tells us?

- **a**. The compositions are identical, and exact mixtures are given.
- **b**. The compositions are identical, but exact mixtures are not given.
- **c**. The compositions differ, and exact mixtures are given.
- **d**. The compositions differ, but exact mixtures are not given.

### 9. The explanation says lipids rarely switch leaflets without enzymes. Which conclusion preserves that claim?

- **a**. Switching without enzymes is impossible.
- **b**. Switching without enzymes is common.
- **c**. Switching without enzymes is uncommon, rather than ruled out.
- **d**. Switching is equally common with or without enzymes.

### 10. Which connection between a membrane feature and its role matches the explanation?

- **a**. Outer-surface sugars form the glycocalyx and support cell recognition.
- **b**. Outer-surface sugars form the glycocalyx and restrain membrane fluidity.
- **c**. Cholesterol forms the glycocalyx and supports cell recognition.
- **d**. Cholesterol forms the glycocalyx and restrains membrane fluidity.

## What changed

| Q | Revised target | Improvement and remaining limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Match both head/tail properties to arrangement | All choices describe arrangement; no redundant inward/tail-core alternatives. Still a basic recognition item. |
| 2 | Correct a claim about the whole molecule | Parallel head/tail property pairs replace unrelated sugar/protein causes. Still close to definition recall. |
| 3 | Compare packing and movement | All choices combine the same two variables; only one matches the taught relationship. |
| 4 | Apply the definition of fluidity | Compares two hypothetical membranes using movement already defined in the explanation. No new science fact is asserted. |
| 5 | Limit a prediction to available teaching | Separates naming a factor from knowing the direction of its effect; no unsupported chain-length rule is introduced. |
| 6 | Correct an overgeneralization about cholesterol | Requires both conditions; no narrower true alternative competes with the full buffering description. |
| 7 | Compare model representations | Both drawings contain the same mixture, so the learner must distinguish fixed versus sideways-moving components. |
| 8 | Recognize asymmetry without invented detail | Separates known composition differences from unknown exact mixtures. Tests evidence boundaries, not a new mechanism. |
| 9 | Preserve the qualifier rare | Replaces enzyme-name recall with the distinction between uncommon and impossible switching. |
| 10 | Connect a feature to its role | All options pair feature, structure, and role; no correct-choice length giveaway. Primarily role recognition. |
